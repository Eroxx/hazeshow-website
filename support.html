Support

Hazeshow lights a live show for your music, on your iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple TV. Here are answers to the usual questions.

Ask other Hazeshow users and me on the Discord: questions in #help, bugs in #bug-reports, wishes in #ideas.

Join the Discord

Still stuck? Write to support@hazeshow.com.

The lights aren't moving

Hazeshow needs to hear the music. On a Mac, the first time, macOS asks to let it hear your Mac's sound: allow it in System Settings, Privacy & Security, Screen & System Audio Recording. Then check Settings → Sound is set to the sound playing on this Mac.

On iPhone and iPad, Hazeshow listens through the microphone, so play the music out loud nearby; allow the microphone in Settings, Hazeshow if you turned it down. (iOS lets no app hear what other apps play, except through a screen broadcast: Hazeshow's Settings, Sound.)

No music handy?

Hazeshow comes with a song of its own, First Light. The welcome starts with it (Start the Show); after that, play it from Settings on iPhone and iPad, or ⇧D on a Mac: the show tours every stage, a card naming each. On an Apple TV, choose Watch the Show and the TV puts it on by itself.

How do I get it on my TV?

Install Hazeshow on your Apple TV and open it, on the same Wi-Fi as your iPhone, iPad or Mac. Then:

  • iPhone or iPad: tap the show to bring up the controls, tap Apple TV, and choose your TV.
  • Mac: move the mouse over the show and click Show on Apple TV in the card at the bottom left (or press D, or choose it in the Show menu).

Turn on Play the music on the TV to hear the music from the TV, in time with the lights. On the TV itself, How to Connect shows these steps, and Watch the Show plays the demo with nothing to set up; press MENU to come back.

Do I need a Mac?

No. Hazeshow on iPhone and iPad runs the show itself, and sends it to an Apple TV. On a Mac you can also send your iPhone's sound to the Mac with AirPlay (turn on AirPlay Receiver in System Settings, General, AirDrop & Handoff), and Hazeshow hears it like anything else the Mac plays.

Can it be my screen saver?

On a Mac, yes, one that follows the music: in Settings → Window, choose Show the music after a few minutes. With music playing and no mouse or keys for that long, the show fills the screen; touch anything to get your desktop back.

Shortcuts and Siri

Hazeshow's actions are in the Shortcuts app on iPhone, iPad and Mac: play the demo song, choose a room, next room, show on Apple TV. Ask Siri, for example, to “Show Hazeshow on the TV”.

The lights aren't quite on the beat

On iPhone and iPad, pause your music and tap Time the Lights to the Beat in Settings. Hazeshow plays a beat for about 20 seconds, times its own lights against it, and sets them right for every song; then the screen flashes with each kick so you can see them land. If they still look a little off, Earlier and Later on the same card move them 10 ms at a time. Song files played through AirPods or a Bluetooth speaker are allowed for by themselves.

On a Mac, choose Sync the Lights with the Sound in Settings → Sound and tap along to a beat, or set Delay the lights by hand there. For a TV, use Sync the TV with the Sound (iPhone and iPad) or TV lag in Settings → Apple TV (Mac).

How do I pick the stage?

On iPhone and iPad, swipe across the show or tap Stage. On a Mac, press G to step through the stages, click the stage's name on the corner card, or choose in Settings → Show. Automatic picks from the song's genre; choose it at the top of the list (or press ⇧G on a Mac) to go back to it.

Can I keep a look I like?

Yes: add it to Favourites. It keeps the stage and colours on screen (with the truss shape and beam move). Favourites stay until you delete them and never play by themselves: pick one any time to bring it back, and the show stays on it until you choose Automatic again. On iPhone and iPad, tap Favourites under the show; on a Mac, press Z to add one, Q to bring them back in turn, and R to let the autopilot back. Press ? to see every key.
